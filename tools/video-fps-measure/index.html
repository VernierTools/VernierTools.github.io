How it works
The frame list is read straight from the file, and frames are decoded without playing the video, so the display's refresh rate doesn't matter. When the same picture repeats (3-2-3-2… for 24 fps in 60 fps), the frame rate of the content is how often the picture actually changes.
What it can't tell
Scenes with no motion at all are skipped. Videos converted by blending or interpolating frames have no repeated pictures, so they read as the file's own rate. Animation drawn on twos shows the rate the drawings change. ProRes, DNxHD, QuickTime Animation and some other codecs can't be decoded in a browser; only the timestamps are checked for those. MOV files with H.264, HEVC, PNG or Motion JPEG are fully analyzed.
