How it works

The brightest area of the sky is taken as the sun. Its centre is found from the light-weighted average of that area (with the stretching of the equirectangular image corrected), and turned into the rotation each program expects.

Good to know
  • The HDRI must be equirectangular (2:1, the usual “lat-long” format). Radiance .hdr and OpenEXR (scanline; no compression, RLE, ZIPS or ZIP) can be read. For EXR with PIZ or DWA compression, use the .hdr version or re-save with ZIP.
  • The rotations assume each program shows the HDRI the default way: Blender's Environment Texture, Unreal's HDRI Backdrop, Unity's Skybox/Panoramic material and an Arnold Skydome Light in Maya. If you rotated the HDRI there, type that angle in the card.
  • Unreal and Maya can orient an HDRI differently depending on the version and the way it is set up (a Sky Light cubemap, other renderers, HDRP's HDRI Sky in Unity). Check the shadow direction once the first time.
  • On overcast skies, indoors or at night there is no clear sun, so the brightest spot may be a lamp or a window. Click the right place in the image.
  • Very large files (16K and above) may not fit in the browser's memory. 8K or smaller is safest.
