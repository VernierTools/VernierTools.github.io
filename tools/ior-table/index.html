About the values
  • IOR is the value at 589.3 nm (yellow sodium light), the usual reference wavelength. It shifts slightly with wavelength and temperature; conditions are noted when the source gives them, otherwise room temperature.
  • F0 is the reflectance of the surface seen head-on, against air. The Abbe number measures dispersion (the rainbow split in glass and gems): the smaller the number, the stronger the split.
  • Birefringent crystals (quartz, sapphire, calcite…) have two indices; the table shows the ordinary ray and lists the other one as “e”.
  • Natural gems vary from stone to stone, so they show a range and its middle as the representative value.
  • “CG presets” are not measurements of the material itself but the values Blender uses by default, and those of Gullstrand's model eye.
  • Metals: computed from spectral n and k (380–780 nm) under D65 into linear sRGB. Base Color is the head-on reflectance; Edge Tint is the reflectance at 82° divided by Schlick's approximation — how Blender defines its F82 Tint. IOR and Extinction are solved per R, G and B so that they give the same reflectance at 0° and 82°, so Physical Conductor and F82 Tint look nearly identical. Gold is more saturated than sRGB can show, so its red is clipped to 1.
  • Data: refractiveindex.info database (CC0), unless noted otherwise.

A renderer's IOR assumes the object sits in air. Where two materials touch — a bubble in water, water in a glass — the surface between them should use the ratio of the two indices instead.
A result below 1 is fine: it means the inside is optically thinner than the outside (an air bubble in water is 0.750).
The critical angle applies to light travelling from the higher-index side toward the lower one; beyond it, all light is reflected. At the Brewster angle, reflected light is fully polarized.
Edit any of the three; the others follow. F0 = ((IOR − 1) ÷ (IOR + 1))², for a surface in air.
“Specular” is the 0–1 slider used by Unreal Engine, the Disney BRDF and Blender's Principled BSDF before 4.0, where 0.5 means F0 = 0.04 (IOR 1.5). In Blender 4.0 and later, enter the IOR directly.
